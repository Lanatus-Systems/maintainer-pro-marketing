# Website for tuition centre: batches, timings and an enquiry button

**Quick answer:** A tuition centre website only needs one clear page: the classes and subjects you teach, your batch timings, where you are, a little proof (results you can show, years of teaching), and a button so a parent can call or message you. The hard part isn't building it; it's keeping batch timings and admission dates correct every term. With AasaanWeb you describe the page, or a change, in plain words, check it on a preview, and publish it yourself. Small changes go live in about 10 minutes.

## Why a tuition centre needs a page at all
Parents ask around, then they search. When they look up your centre's name, they want to check three things before they call: which classes and subjects you take, when the batches run, and whether you are close enough. If that answer sits only in a WhatsApp status or a pamphlet, they move on to the next name. One page that answers those questions does the job, and you can share the same link in WhatsApp groups and on your Google listing.

## What to put on your tuition centre page
1. **A headline that says what and where.** For example: "Maths and Science tuition for Class 8–10, near [your area]."
2. **Classes, subjects and boards** you teach, in short lines.
3. **Batch timings**, by class, and which days. Say clearly when a new batch starts.
4. **Location and hours**: address, landmark, and when parents can visit or call.
5. **Proof**: a real photo of the classroom, how many years you've taught, results you can show (only real ones).
6. **Common questions**: demo class, batch size, how to join. Answer them once on the page instead of on every call.
7. **An enquiry button**: call, or a WhatsApp chat, placed where people see it first on a phone.

Fees: many centres put fees on the page, many prefer "call for details". Either works; just keep whatever you show correct.

## The real problem: timings change every term
Batches shift with school timings, exams and holidays. A page that still shows last term's timings sends parents to the wrong slot, and that costs you admissions. Usually the fix waits on whoever built the site.

Every change shows on a preview first, the way a parent will see it. Asking for a change doesn't put it live by itself; you publish when it looks right. Small changes go live in about 10 minutes, and you don't need to know how websites are built.

For most single-location tuition and coaching centres, one page is enough: parents want classes, timings, place and a number.
