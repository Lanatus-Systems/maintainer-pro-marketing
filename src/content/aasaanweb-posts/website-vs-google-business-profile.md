# Website vs Google Business Profile: what an Indian shop needs

If you run a shop, salon, clinic or coaching centre in India, you have probably been told two things: "Get on Google Maps" and "Make a website." It is fair to ask whether you need both, or whether one is enough.

The short answer: they do different jobs. A Google Business Profile helps people find you. A website helps them decide to choose you, and gives you full control over what they read. Most local businesses do best with both, linked to each other.

Here is how to think about it.

**Quick answer:** A Google Business Profile helps people find you on Google Search and Maps, with your phone number, hours and reviews. A website helps them decide to choose you, and gives you full control over what they read. Most local businesses in India do best with both, linked to each other and showing the same details.

## What a Google Business Profile does well

A Google Business Profile is the listing that appears in Google Search and Google Maps when someone looks up your business name or searches for something like "saree shop near me". It is free to create and manage through Google.

It is very good at a few things:

- **Being found on Maps.** Your pin, address and directions appear where people are already searching.
- **Quick facts.** Phone number, opening hours and a call button sit right in the search result.
- **Reviews.** Customers can leave star ratings and comments, which many people read before visiting.
- **Photos.** You and your customers can add pictures of the place.

If you do not have a profile yet, setting one up is one of the most useful things you can do this week. Claim your listing, verify it, and fill in every field honestly: category, hours, phone, address and photos.

## Where a Google Business Profile falls short

The profile is useful, but it is not yours in the way a website is.

- **Limited space to explain.** You get a short description and a few fields. It is hard to show your full range, explain a service properly, or answer common questions in detail.
- **Google decides the layout.** You cannot choose what appears first, how it looks, or what a visitor sees next to your listing.
- **Other people can edit it.** Users can suggest changes to details like your hours or category, and incorrect suggestions sometimes go through.
- **It sits next to competitors.** Search results and Maps often show nearby businesses right beside yours.
- **It is hard to share as a full introduction.** Sending a Maps link on WhatsApp tells someone where you are, but not much about why they should come.

## What a website does well

A website is a page you control. On it, you decide the words, the photos, the order and the main action you want visitors to take.

- **Your full story in your words.** What you sell, what makes you different, who you serve, what to expect on a first visit.
- **Products and services in detail.** A menu, a list of courses, a range of parts, a set of packages, laid out clearly.
- **Answers to common questions.** Parking, home delivery, payment options, how to book, what to bring.
- **One link for everything.** Put it in your WhatsApp Business profile, Instagram bio, visiting cards and on the Google profile itself.
- **A clear call to action.** A big call or WhatsApp button, placed where you want it.

A good small business website does not need many pages. For most shops, one clear landing page is enough.

## Where a website falls short

A website on its own does not automatically bring people from Google Maps. Local search results lean heavily on Business Profiles. That is why the two work best together rather than as a choice between them.

A website also needs to be kept current. If your page shows old timings while your Google profile shows new ones, customers get confused.

## Side by side

| | Google Business Profile | Your own website |
|---|---|---|
| Helps people find you on Maps | Yes, this is its main job | Not directly |
| You control layout and wording | Only within Google's fields | Fully |
| Room to explain products and services | Limited | As much as you need |
| Shows reviews | Yes | Only what you choose to add |
| Can be edited by others | Users can suggest edits | Only by you |
| Works as one link to share | Partly | Yes |

## How to use them together

Think of the Google Business Profile as the signboard on the main road, and the website as the conversation at your counter. One brings people in; the other helps them decide.

1. **Set up and verify your Google Business Profile.** Fill in every field, add real photos, and keep hours up to date.
2. **Make a simple website.** One page with what you sell, where you are, your hours, photos and clear contact buttons.
3. **Link them both ways.** Add your website address in the website field of your Google profile. Show your Maps location or address on your website.
4. **Keep the facts identical.** Same name, phone number, address and hours in both places. Mismatched details look careless and confuse customers.
5. **Update both when something changes.** Festival timings, a new number, a new branch. Change both on the same day.

## Which one should you start with?

If you have neither, start with the Google Business Profile, because it is the quickest way to appear when people search nearby. Then add a website so that the people who find you have somewhere to learn more and get in touch.

If you already have a profile with good reviews, a website is the natural next step. It gives you space to explain what the listing cannot, and it is one link you can share everywhere.
