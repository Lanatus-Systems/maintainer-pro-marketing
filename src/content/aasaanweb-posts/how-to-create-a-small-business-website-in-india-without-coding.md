# How to create a website for your small business in India without coding

Most small business owners in India do not need a big website. You need one clear page that tells a customer what you do, where you are, when you are open, and how to reach you. That page should be easy to change when your prices, photos or timings change.

This guide walks through the whole thing in plain steps. You will not need to write code, and you will not need to learn design software.

## Step 1: Decide what the page is for

Before you think about colours or fonts, answer one question: what should a visitor do after reading your page?

For most local businesses the answer is one of these:

- Call you or send a WhatsApp message
- Visit the shop, clinic or studio
- Book a slot or a class
- Ask for a quote or a price list

Pick the one that matters most. Everything on the page should make that action easy. A salon wants bookings. A coaching class wants parents to call. A hardware supplier wants enquiries with a part number. Write your answer down in one line, because you will use it again.

## Step 2: Collect what you already have

You already have most of what a good page needs. Spend twenty minutes gathering it in one place, such as a note on your phone:

- **Business name** and one line that says what you sell and where. For example: "Fresh cakes and custom orders in Indiranagar, Bengaluru."
- **Phone and WhatsApp number** that someone actually answers.
- **Address** and a landmark people recognise.
- **Opening hours**, including the weekly off day and any half days.
- **4 to 8 real photos**: the shop front, the counter, your work, your team. Clear phone photos taken in daylight are better than stock images.
- **Your main products or services**, written the way customers ask for them.
- **What makes you different**: home delivery, same-day service, 20 years in the same lane, a speciality nobody else nearby offers.

If you have a Google Business Profile, an Instagram page or a printed visiting card, look at them too. They often contain wording you have already tested with customers.

## Step 3: Choose how the page gets built

There are three common ways to get a small business website in India. Each has its place.

**Hire a developer or agency.** You can get a polished result, and someone else handles the technical side. The catch is the small changes later. A new photo, a festival offer or a change in Sunday timing means sending a message and waiting for them to find time.

**Use a template website builder.** You pick a template and fill it in yourself. This works if you enjoy setting things up, but you start with an empty layout and have to decide every heading, section and setting on your own, usually while also running the business.

**Describe your business and let the page be made for you.** Tools like AasaanWeb take what you tell them about the business, in plain words, and turn it into a complete landing page. You then look at the page and say what should be different.

Whichever route you take, judge it by two things: how quickly you get a clear first page, and how easy it is to change that page next month.

## Step 4: Write for the customer, not for yourself

When the first version of the page is ready, read it as if you were a stranger standing outside your shop with a phone in your hand. Ask:

- In the first few seconds, can I tell what this business sells and where it is?
- Can I find the phone number or WhatsApp button without scrolling forever?
- Are the opening hours correct today?
- Do the photos look like the real place?

Use simple, specific words. "Same-day blouse stitching, ready by 7 pm" says more than "Quality tailoring services." If customers ask you the same question every day, answer it on the page.

## Step 7: Keep it up to date

A website that shows last year's prices or old timings does more harm than good. Customers who arrive at a closed shutter rarely come back.

Make a habit of checking the page whenever something changes:

- New product or service added, or one dropped
- Festival or seasonal timing
- New photos of recent work
- A change in phone number or address

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Too many pages.** A single clear page often beats five thin ones. Start with one and add more only when customers need it.
- **No clear action.** If the call or WhatsApp button is hidden, visitors leave.
- **Stock photos only.** Real photos of your shop and work build trust faster.
- **Outdated hours.** This is the most common complaint about local business information online.
- **Copying a big brand's website.** Large companies have different goals. Your page needs to answer a local customer's practical questions.
