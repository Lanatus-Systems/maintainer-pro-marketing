# Website for your salon business: what to put on the page

**Quick answer:** A salon website usually only needs one clear page: which services you offer, your open hours, where you are, a few true photos of the work, and a button so a customer can call, book or message you on WhatsApp. The hard part is keeping timings, festival closures and new services correct. ## Why a salon page beats a visiting card and a WhatsApp status

Customers search your salon name, a service, or the area before they call. They want three answers fast: do you do what they need, are you open when they can come, and how do they reach you. If that answer lives only on a visiting card, an Instagram highlight, or a WhatsApp status, they try the next parlour. One page you can share on WhatsApp and on your Google listing does that job every day.

## What to put on a salon landing page

1. **A headline that says who and where.** For example: "Hair and beauty salon in [your area]" or "Bridal makeup and party looks, near [landmark]."
2. **Services in short lines.** Haircut, colour, spa, cleanup, bridal package — only what you actually offer. Remove a service the day you stop it.
3. **Hours and holidays.** Weekday and weekend timings, lunch break if you have one, and a clear line when you are closed for a festival or staff leave.
4. **Location.** Address, landmark, and a simple map link if you already have one elsewhere; customers care that the pin matches the door.
5. **Proof.** Real photos of the salon, the work, or the team. Only photos you are allowed to show.
6. **Common questions.** First visit, how long a colour takes, whether you take walk-ins, parking. Answer them once on the page instead of on every call.
7. **A call, book or WhatsApp button** where people see it first on a phone — the same advice our WhatsApp guide gives shop owners.

Rates: some salons list them, many prefer "call for details". Either is fine; keep whatever you show accurate. Do not invent packages you do not run.

## The real problem: offers and timings go stale

Bridal season rates, a new colour service, Saturday evening slots, Diwali closures — these change often. A page that still shows last year's hours or an old festival offer sends customers to a closed door or a wrong price. Usually the fix waits on whoever built the site, through messages, screenshots and another wait.

On AasaanWeb, the pieces that go stale first are the ones you change yourself: headlines and offers, photos, hours, address and phone, prices and services, buttons, and FAQ or proof sections. You say it the way you would say it out loud, for example:

- "Saturday evening slots start at 5, not 4."
- "Add keratin treatment under services."
- "The phone number in the footer is old. Use the new one."
- "Put the WhatsApp button above the fold on phones."
